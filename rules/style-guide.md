# Style Guide

Learned from the real College of Nursing news posts. Update this file whenever more examples are added to `examples/`.

This guide WINS over the examples when they differ. The last section, "Learned from the owner's edits", wins over everything else in this guide, because those rules come from the owner's own corrections. Real published posts are often shorter (1 to 5 short paragraphs) and some break these rules; the target here is still 3 to 4 paragraphs, third person, no title repeated inside the body.

## Voice (all content, both languages)
- Academic-institutional: the register of an international university website. Scholarly but plain, precise, objective and measured. Not promotional, not literary, not chatty, and not a dense research paper.
- Follow the academic writing guides (`academic/en.md` for English, `academic/ar.md` for Arabic) for vocabulary, sentence shape and what to avoid.
- Third person, objective, factual. The university speaks about itself as "the college", "the Deanship", "the university". Never "we" or "you".
- Use the discipline's own terms (clinical training, laboratory session, curriculum, learning outcomes, accreditation) and measured verbs (aims to, contributes to, forms part of).
- No rhetorical devices: no rhymed prose (سجع), no rhetorical questions, no metaphors, no slogans.
- Respectful toward staff. Always keep the academic title with a name, exactly as in the source (Dr., Assistant Professor Dr., Professor Dr.).
- No hype, no slogans, no emojis, no hashtags, no exclamation marks, no direct appeals like "come join us".
- Past tense for events that happened. Announcements of upcoming events use a plain future or present form.

## News post structure (College News)
Title:
- Short, informative, sentence case, no final full stop, usually 3 to 8 words.
- Patterns seen: "A segment of the practical training for students", "Tour of the final exams", "Honouring the top students", "Educational activity on healthy food", "Civil defence and occupational safety workshop", "Visit of Dr. [Name], Assistant Dean".

Body: plain paragraphs only, no headings, no lists. Normally 3 to 4 paragraphs following the structure below, even when the owner gave only a title.
1. What happened: the activity, which students or staff, where, as part of what course or requirement, under whose supervision.
2. How it was carried out and what it gave the participants (applied knowledge, skills, experience).
3. The Deanship's statement: the activity falls under the guidance of a named official and within the college's plan or quality standards.
4. The aim: what the activity develops (skills, teamwork, professional discipline) and the setting, such as healthcare institutions in Holy Karbala.

Wording seen in real posts that is worth reusing when the facts support it: "under the supervision of", "within the framework of its plan to", "in line with quality standards", "in Holy Karbala", "the Deanship of the College of Nursing, Warith Al-Anbiyaa University confirmed that".
Use this kind of general wording freely, even if the owner did not state it, and list those sentences in the review notes. Never name an official, give a number, a date or a venue that the owner did not provide.

Length: about 120 to 220 words in English. Arabic equal in content.

Sub Content: leave empty for news posts. The Content box is used instead.

## Page content (website manager)
- Very short and label-like. Examples from the site: the value "Integrity" with the line "Commitment to honesty, ethical principles and academic transparency"; the portal card "Policies" with the line "Approved policies and regulations".
- Title: one or two words (or a short phrase). Description: one line, no final full stop, no more than about 12 words.
- Same formal tone. Use the exact wording the owner supplies for official items such as laws, policies and plans. Do not paraphrase official texts without saying so.

## Arabic specifics
- Academic Modern Standard Arabic (فصحى أكاديمية), short to medium sentences. No colloquial or Iraqi dialect words.
- Measured academic connectors used sparingly (وفي هذا السياق، ويأتي ذلك في إطار، بما ينسجم مع). Avoid stock phrases such as ومن الجدير بالذكر، علاوة على ذلك، في ظل.
- Use the official Arabic names of colleges and units exactly as on the website.
- Use Arabic punctuation (، ؛ ؟) and keep titles free of a final full stop.
- The Arabic and English versions are written separately but must contain the same facts, names and numbers.

## English specifics
- Academic-institutional British English: organised, programme, centre, honour, defence, analyse. Keep official names exactly as listed.
- No contractions, no question marks, no exclamation marks.
- Mostly active voice for descriptions, passive acceptable for events ("training was conducted").
- Official English names exactly as in the dashboard: "College of Nursing", "Warith Al-Anbiyaa University".
